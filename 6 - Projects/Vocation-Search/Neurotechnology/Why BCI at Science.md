Dear Science Corporation Team,

Howdy!

I’m writing to express my deep interest in contributing to your work at the intersection of neuroscience, engineering, and human experience. As someone driven by a lifelong fascination with cognition and a hands-on builder’s mindset, I see Science not just as a company, but as the kind of launchpad I’ve been searching for, a place where rigorous tools and ambitious ideas come together to push the frontier of neurotechnology.

I come from a biomedical engineering background with broad exposure to signal processing, circuits, and software development. This generalist training shaped my comfort at the seams of disciplines. In my senior capstone project, I led the development of JARVIS, an AI-powered documentation assistant. Despite having no formal web development training, I took point on both technical architecture and group leadership, ultimately producing a tool that earned strong praise from faculty in an unfamiliar domain.

I’m currently building SCOOL, a platform that blends knowledge management and cognitive scaffolding to support lifelong learning. Through that, I’ve interviewed people across a variety of unrelated disciplines, all the while trying to steer a unified vision, a challenge I’ve found both humbling and energizing. That work has shown me the power (and limits) of bootstrapped systems, and is a big part of what’s pushing me to seek more stable support and mentorship in a space like BCI.

I’m especially drawn to Science’s full-stack approach, from MEMS chips to deep learning interfaces, because it allows people like me to build on solid foundations instead of working in silos. I want to learn everything I can about the BCI stack and contribute wherever I’m most useful. Whether that’s helping connect software and hardware teams, translating research needs into design specs, or finding overlooked points of friction in the user experience, I want to be there.

My passion for neurotech comes from both a technical and personal place. Living with ADHD and anxiety, I’ve spent the last few years creating my own tools to support memory, focus, and task management. None of them are perfect, but the process of designing for my own mind taught me what matters when cognition falters, and why agency must be preserved as we build tools that interface more directly with the brain.

Ultimately, I want to help build systems that restore and expand what it means to think, to remember, and to connect. I’d be honored to learn from and contribute to the team at Science.

Warmly,
