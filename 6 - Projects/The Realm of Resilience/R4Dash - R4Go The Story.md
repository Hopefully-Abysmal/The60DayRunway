My name is Landon Dahle, but in creative circles I go by Elryan The Explorer. I’m one of the co-developers behind the **R4Dash** and **R4Go** initiatives, and I’d like to share how this project came to life.

It started when I saw the incredible work Dan Van Atta had done with [WNC Supply Sites](https://wnc-supply-sites.com/)—a lightweight coordination platform built in response to disaster logistics chaos. Dan saw that supplies weren’t reaching where they were most needed, and built a tool to help fix that. It worked—really well.

But as I saw how people engaged with that system, something else became clear:

**There are _so many_ people willing to volunteer—skilled, ready, and motivated—but they have no idea where their time could make the most difference.**

The problem isn’t just about moving supplies.  
It’s about **activating people**.

We need better coordination not only for logistics, but for **human effort**.

That’s what sparked the idea behind **R4Dash** and **R4Go**.

What if we could apply the same logic that WNC used for supply chain redistribution…  
to **people**?

What if we had a way to:

- Show volunteers what needs exist in real time
    
- Match them with tasks based on location, skills, and urgency
    
- Track and reward their contributions to grow trust and autonomy
    
- Make participation _feel doable_ and even _fun_—especially in overwhelming times
    

So I partnered with R4 to develop this modular extension of WNC Supply Sites.  
It starts with disaster response—but it doesn't end there.

R4Dash is the dashboard for organizers.  
R4Go is the street-level app for action.  

Together, they’re designed to make **volunteering scalable, transparent, and rewarding**—even across languages, devices, and disconnected zones.

We’re designing with two realities in mind:

- 🆘 **Disaster Mode**: where bandwidth is low and urgency is high
    
- 🌤 **Sunny Sky Mode**: where we can practice, improve, and build long-term civic infrastructure
    

Our mission is to turn **goodwill into impact**—at scale.

If you're part of the Aid Arena network—or just someone passionate about community resilience—we’re looking for people to help co-develop, test, and refine this platform with us.

Let’s make it easier to _show up_ for each other—no matter the storm.
