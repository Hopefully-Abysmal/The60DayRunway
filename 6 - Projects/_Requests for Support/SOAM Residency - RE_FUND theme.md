### 🟨 **Why are you interested in our annual theme?**

Venture capital, data harvesting, and growth-at-all-costs have warped how we build and fund technology. I’m interested in RE:FUND because it directly challenges these paradigms, inviting a space where we can prototype sustainable, equitable, and non-extractive funding models. I believe that in order for knowledge, governance, and digital infrastructure to serve the public interest, we need new institutional architectures—ones that fund _value_ instead of _virality_. This theme resonates deeply with my work to reimagine education not as a product but as a commons—where creators are compensated based on societal impact, not algorithmic reach.

---

### 🟨 **How does this topic connect to your current and previous work?**

I’m currently building out a framework called **SCOOL** (the Scalable Community for Open Online Learning), a decentralized platform that tracks and rewards user-generated educational content based on its long-term utility. This follows years of interacting with the education system in both standard and higher education contexts along with significant exposure to and utilization of the modern day social media platforms and their user generated content business models. Focused on ethical AI and personalized knowledge systems, my work lives at the intersection of epistemology, design, and survival—experimenting with how we build self-sustaining, transparent systems that preserve dignity and autonomy. The RE:FUND theme matches my trajectory of creating anti-extractive alternatives for creators and learners who are often left behind by current funding ecosystems.

---

### 🟨 **How do you hope to benefit from participating in the SOAM Residency program?**

I want to refine the economic and institutional logic behind SCOOL in collaboration with experts in public interest finance, governance, and digital equity. I’m especially eager to stress-test SCOOL’s **impact compensation model** with peers from diverse disciplines, gain feedback on our **reputation-weighted governance**, and explore interoperability with other models being developed at SOAM. I also seek mentorship and accountability as I formalize a working prototype and prepare it for real-world pilot programs. Most importantly, I’m looking for kindred builders who believe the system can—and must—be redesigned.

[[SCOOL and Impact Compensation Project Proposal]]

### **Project Title**

**The Scalable Community for Open Online Learning (SCOOL) :: A Public Infrastructure for Knowledge Compensation Through Impact**

---

### **Objective**

SCOOL aims to prototype a visionary **non-extractive funding model for education** that rewards creators based on the **long-term public impact of their contributions**, not their marketability. It reorients compensation around **effort, time, and memory integration**—transforming how knowledge work is funded and valued. In doing so, it provides a blueprint for scalable, ethical alternatives to ad-driven or credential-based education platforms.

---

### **Challenge**

Today’s dominant educational and creator platforms monetize **engagement over enlightenment**. Creators are forced to optimize for virality, while learners are reduced to consumers. Funding mechanisms reward short-term clicks and brand partnerships rather than **measurable, enduring public benefit**. There is no sustainable pathway for educators, researchers, or knowledge workers to thrive in a commons-based system without selling access, data, or self-sovereignty.

---

### **Proposition**

SCOOL introduces an **Impact Attribution and Compensation Protocol** that tracks how user-generated educational content affects others—through memory retention, derivative works, citations, and propagation over time. Rather than minting speculative tokens, SCOOL uses a **livable wage pool** funded by subscriptions and grants, distributed monthly based on **Impact Scores**.

![[Funding Cycle for Open Access Education With Accelerative Learning Tools.png]]

Ideally this platform would follow the funding model above and be managed by a community owned Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO) with levers marked in purple. Profits can be made by third parties through the development of accelerative learning tools to be explored simultaneously during this fellowship (marked in orange).

Key innovations include:

- **Time-as-tribute** model: users’ time is logged (not traded) and weighted by comprehension and reuse.
- **Semantic impact engine**: traces knowledge flow through citation chains, remixes, and user vocabulary shifts.
- **Transparent audit logs**: all distributions, weights, and formulas are published for verification.
- **Creator tiers**: participants choose how their intellectual property enters the commons, with proportional tradeoffs in reward and reuse.

This model **redistributes power from platforms to contributors**, reframes **value as verifiable transformation**, and treats education as a **public utility**, not a private product.

---

### **Output**

The final prototype will consist of:

- A **codebase** implementing the core **impact attribution engine** and **smart contract logic** (if on-chain tracking is pursued),
- A **visual mockup** of the user interface for learners and creators,
- A **public-facing whitepaper or microsite** explaining the funding logic and governance principles,
- An **interactive dashboard** showcasing simulated or real impact calculations.

This combination ensures accessibility for both expert audiences (researchers, policymakers, developers) and general publics seeking models of fairer, future-oriented institutions.
